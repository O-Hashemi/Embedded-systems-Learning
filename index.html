Project Overview

The Squirtle Smart Garden is an ESP32-powered automated watering system designed to monitor environmental conditions and irrigate plants automatically based on real-time soil dynamics.

The system continuously tracks ambient temperature, relative humidity, and fluid reservoir levels while managing a low-voltage DC water pump via a dedicated MOSFET switching module.

This project integrates 3D mechanical modeling, embedded hardware design, custom PCB soldering, microcontroller programming, and full-stack web development into a unified engineering solution.

Design Journey

How the project evolved from the original concept into the final Squirtle design.

Original Ladybug Design

The project originally began as a Ladybug-themed smart garden. The concept was creative, but once I started planning the physical build, I ran into severe space limitations. There simply wasn't enough room inside the shell to hold the ESP32, the wiring, and a functional water reservoir without needing an awkward secondary tank. This prototype highlighted the need for a much more spacious and practical design.

Design Evaluation

After reviewing the limitations of the ladybug layout, I knew I needed a major upgrade. I focused the redesign on three main goals: increasing internal space, making component placement cleaner, and ensuring long-term maintainability. This phase taught me how to take a fun concept and adapt it into a realistic engineering solution.

Final Squirtle Design

The final Squirtle design provided the perfect sweet spot for fluid targeting. By leveraging the forward-facing anatomy of the character, I was able to direct the water line precisely into the center of the plant pot where the root zone needs it most. This completely solved my previous placement issues and created a highly reliable, standalone smart garden!

System Architecture

The ESP32 acts as the main controller for the sensors, pump system, and dashboard.

How It Works

• Environmental Telemetry: An onboard sensor array continuously measures ambient temperature and relative humidity.

• Fluid Dynamics Monitoring: A dedicated water-level sensor monitors the reservoir to protect the pump from dry-run conditions.

• Embedded Control Logic: The ESP32 processes real-time sensor inputs and executes closed-loop control algorithms to actuate the pump via a MOSFET module.

• IoT Web Interface: The microcontroller acts as a lightweight web server, transmitting real-time system diagnostics to a responsive browser-based dashboard for remote monitoring.

Prototype Testing Analysis

Real-world hardware constraints encountered during initial assembly tests.

Mechanical & Fluid Dynamics Bottlenecks

During physical prototyping, initial system integration testing highlighted three critical design constraints:

• Center of Mass & Instability: As shown here, the cantilevered chassis design shifted the net center of mass drastically outside the footprint of the plant pot. The combined weight of the pump and electronics created an aggressive overturning moment during solo mounts.

• The Temporary Solution: To keep the prototype stable for electrical validation, I 3D-printed a custom mechanical stabilizing hook (visible extending to the left rim of the pot). While this successfully counteracted the load, testing proved that a total structural overhaul is ideal for a production model.

• Fluid Routing & Nozzle Geometry: In an earlier iteration, I designed a V-shaped funnel at the bottom to direct the water. However, physical testing revealed that the funnel geometry restricted flow, caused clogging risks, and misdirected water away from the root core. This drove the pivot to the Squirtle chassis, utilizing its natural layout to drop water cleanly into the center of the soil layout.
