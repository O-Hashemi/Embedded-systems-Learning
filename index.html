Project Overview

The Squirtle Smart Garden is an ESP32 powered automated watering system. It is designed to monitor environmental conditions and water plants automatically when needed.

The system is designed to monitor temperature, humidity, and water level while controlling a pump through a MOSFET module.

This project combines 3D printing, electronics, soldering, programming, and web development into one complete engineering project.

Design Journey

How the project evolved from the original concept into the final Squirtle design.

Original Ladybug Design

The project originally began as a Ladybug-themed smart garden. The concept was creative but very limited. The goal was to create a compact system that could sit directly ontop of a plant and water it from a reservoir. While the design was visually appealing, due to the lack of space we would have needed a second unit become a water tank. The design made it very difficult for maintenance and highlighted the need for a spacious more practical design.

Design Evaluation

After reviewing my first design I knew improvement was needed. I identified several limitation related to internal space, component placement, and long-term maintainability. To improve reliability and simplify the assembly, I redesigned the project with a focus on a connected water storage, and improved accessibility. THis redesign phase helped transform the concept into a more practical engineering solution.

Final Squirtle Design

The final Squirtle design provided just the right amount of room to fit my device, while maintaining an engaging appearance. The shell was designed to function as a water reservoir, alloweing the pump, sensors, electronics, and wiring to be contained inside the head. THe result is a more reliable, scalable, and user friendly smart garden capable eof supporting a wide variety of plants.

System Architecture

The ESP32 acts as the main controller for the sensors, pump system, and dashboard.

How It Works

The temperature and humidity sensor measures the surrounding environment.

The water level sensor checks if the reservoir has enough water.

The ESP32 processes the sensor readings and controls the pump based on the system logic.

The website displays the system status so the project can be monitored through a browser.
