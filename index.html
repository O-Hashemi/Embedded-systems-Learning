Project Overview

The Squirtle Smart Garden is an automated watering system I built using an ESP32. The main goal was to create a device that keeps an eye on the surrounding environment and waters my plants automatically when the soil gets too dry.

The system tracks temperature, humidity, and the water level in the tank, and uses a MOSFET module to safely turn a small water pump on and off when needed.

This project was an awesome way for me to combine a bunch of different skills, including 3D printing, circuit design, soldering, programming, and web development into one working project.

Design Journey

How the project evolved from a ladybug concept into the final Squirtle design.

Original Ladybug Design

The project originally started out as a Ladybug-themed smart garden. It was a fun idea, but once I started trying to fit the physical pieces together, I ran into a massive space problem. There just wasn't enough room inside the shell to hold the ESP32, the messy wiring, and a water tank without needing a separate, awkward external bucket. It quickly taught me that I needed a much bigger and more practical design.

Design Evaluation

After realizing the ladybug layout wouldn't work, I decided to do a major redesign. I focused on three main goals: giving myself way more internal space, making the wiring path cleaner, and making it easier to fix if something went wrong. This phase was all about taking a fun creative concept and tweaking it so it actually works in the real world.

Final Squirtle Design

The final Squirtle design ended up being the perfect sweet spot for fluid targeting. By using the natural shape of the character, I could easily aim the water line straight into the center of the pot where the roots actually need it. This completely solved my layout issues and gave me a reliable, all-in-one smart garden!

System Architecture

How the ESP32 controls the sensors, pump system, and dashboard.

How It Works

• Checking the Air: A sensor constantly reads the surrounding temperature and humidity levels.

• Watching the Tank: A float switch checks if there's enough water left in the tank so the pump never runs dry and burns out.

• Brain Power: The ESP32 processes all these sensor numbers and decides exactly when to trigger the pump through the MOSFET module.

• The Dashboard: The ESP32 hosts a simple local web server, which lets me monitor everything happening with the plant live right through a web browser.

Prototyping & Reality Checks

The unexpected challenges I hit when assembling the hardware for the first time.

Fixing Balance & Water Flow Issues

Prototyping is always a bit messy, and testing this setup on an actual plant pot showed me two big flaws I needed to fix:

• It Kept Tipping Over: Because the pump and electronics sit out on the edge, the weight distribution was totally off. The whole thing wanted to tip forward right off the pot. To keep my prototype steady so I could finish testing the code, I quickly modeled and 3D-printed a stabilizing hook (you can see it resting on the left side of the pot rim). It fixed the balance for now, but the next version will have a much wider, centered base.

• The Funnel Failed: Before choosing the Squirtle shape, I actually tried putting a V-shaped funnel at the bottom to direct the water. It turned out to be a terrible idea—the water pooled up, it threatened to clog, and it sprayed water away from the center of the soil. Pivoting to the Squirtle design solved this completely since the water now drops straight out of the front mouth directly onto the roots.
