Project Overview

The Squirtle Smart Garden is an automated watering system built around an ESP32 microcontroller. The goal was to design a system that monitors environmental data and waters the plant automatically when the soil gets dry.

The system tracks temperature, humidity, and the reservoir water level, using a MOSFET module to safely switch a low-voltage water pump on and off based on sensor inputs.

This project allowed me to integrate multiple engineering disciplines, combining 3D printing, circuit assembly, soldering, embedded programming, and web development into one working prototype.

Design Journey

How the project evolved from the initial creative concept into the final functional design.

Original Ladybug Design

The project originally started as a ladybug-themed smart garden. It was a fun creative concept, but once I started planning the physical build, I ran into severe space limitations. There simply wasn't enough room inside the shell to hold the ESP32, the wiring, and a water reservoir without needing an awkward external tank. This initial prototype highlighted the need for a much more spacious and practical layout.

Design Evaluation

After realizing the limitations of the ladybug layout, I shifted focus to a complete redesign. My main goals were to increase the internal space, make the component layout cleaner, and ensure it was easy to maintain. This phase taught me how to take a creative idea and adapt it so it actually functions reliably in the real world.

Final Squirtle Design

The final Squirtle design ended up being the perfect choice for water targeting. By leveraging the natural shape of the character, I could route the water line to drop water precisely into the center of the pot where the root zone needs it most. This completely solved my previous component layout issues and resulted in a reliable, standalone smart garden.

System Architecture

The ESP32 serves as the central controller for managing the sensor data, pump logic, and web communication.

System Operation

• Environmental Monitoring: A dedicated sensor constantly reads the surrounding ambient temperature and humidity levels.

• Reservoir Safety: A float switch checks the water level to ensure the pump never runs dry, protecting the hardware from burning out.

• Control Logic: The ESP32 processes the sensor inputs and uses a MOSFET switching module to safely actuate the water pump when triggered.

• Web Dashboard: The ESP32 hosts a lightweight local web server, letting me monitor the real-time status of the project live through any browser.

Prototyping & Physical Constraints

Real-world mechanical and fluid challenges encountered during initial system testing.

Mechanical & Fluid Dynamics Adjustments

Testing the physical prototype on an actual plant pot brought forward a few unexpected design flaws that required hands-on troubleshooting:

• Weight Distribution Issues: Because the pump and electronics sit out on the edge, the center of mass was off-balance, causing the assembly to tip forward. To keep the system steady for electrical testing, I designed and 3D-printed a stabilizing hook that rests on the left side of the pot rim. While it worked as a temporary fix, the next version will feature a wider, centered base to distribute the weight evenly.

• Funnel Geometry Failure: In an earlier iteration, I tried using a V-shaped funnel at the bottom to direct the water flow. However, testing showed that the funnel restricted the flow, created a clogging risk, and misdirected water away from the soil core. Pivoting to the Squirtle chassis fixed this entirely, as water now drops cleanly out of the front directly onto the roots.

Future Improvements

For the next version of the project, I plan to develop a companion mobile app and design specific internal routing channels within the 3D-printed chassis to cleanly hide all the wiring. I also want to upgrade to a more powerful pump and integrate a liquid nutrient tracking system. Finally, I will be adjusting my 3D printer settings to ensure a completely leak-proof reservoir right off the print bed, removing the need for secondary hot glue or superglue sealants.
