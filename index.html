Project Overview

The Squirtle Smart Garden is an ESP32 powered automated watering system. It is designed to monitor environmental conditions and water plants automatically when needed.

The system is designed to monitor temperature, humidity, and water level while controlling a pump through a MOSFET module.

This project combines 3D printing, electronics, soldering, programming, and web development into one complete engineering project.

Design Journey

How the project evolved from the original concept into the final Squirtle design.

Original Ladybug Design

The project originally started as a ladybug design. The concept was creative but very limited. The overall idea was to have the device rest on top of the plant and water from underneath. Due to the internal space being limited, I had difficulty fitting the tank, pump, wiring, and sensors cleanly while still maintaing a ladybug shape.

Design Evaluation

After reviewing my first design I knew improvement was needed. I personally wanted it to work for every plant not just mine specfically and wanted one object that could store water and the device together. I then began to redesgin the project.

Final Squirtle Design

The Squirtle design provided just the right amount of room to fit my device, and provided a improved visual theme. It is now able to work for any plant and is one object due to the shell being a tank for the water.

System Architecture

The ESP32 acts as the main controller for the sensors, pump system, and dashboard.

How It Works

The temperature and humidity sensor measures the surrounding environment.

The water level sensor checks if the reservoir has enough water.

The ESP32 processes the sensor readings and controls the pump based on the system logic.

The website displays the system status so the project can be monitored through a browser.
