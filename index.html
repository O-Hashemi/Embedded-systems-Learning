Project Overview

The Squirtle Smart Garden is an ESP32 powered automated watering system. It is designed to monitor environmental conditions and water plants automatically when needed.

The system is designed to monitor temperature, humidity, and water level while controlling a pump through a MOSFET module.

This project combines 3D printing, electronics, soldering, programming, and web development into one complete engineering project.

Design Journey

How the project evolved from the original concept into the final Squirtle design.

Original Ladybug Design

The project originally began as a Ladybug-themed smart garden. The concept was creative, but once I started planning the physical build, I ran into severe space limitations. There simply wasn't enough room inside the shell to hold the ESP32, the wiring, and a functional water reservoir without needing an awkward secondary tank. This prototype highlighted the need for a much more spacious and practical design.

Design Evaluation

After reviewing the limitations of the ladybug layout, I knew I needed a major upgrade. I focused the redesign on three main goals: increasing internal space, making component placement cleaner, and ensuring long-term maintainability. This phase taught me how to take a fun concept and adapt it into a realistic engineering solution.

Final Squirtle Design

The final Squirtle design provided the perfect sweet spot. It gave me plenty of room to fit the components while keeping an engaging look. The shell now functions as a built-in water reservoir, allowing the pump, sensors, and wiring to be neatly contained inside the top assembly. The result is a much more reliable, standalone smart garden!

System Architecture

The ESP32 acts as the main controller for the sensors, pump system, and dashboard.

How It Works

The temperature and humidity sensor measures the surrounding environment.

The water level sensor checks if the reservoir has enough water.

The ESP32 processes the sensor readings and controls the pump based on the system logic.

The website displays the system status so the project can be monitored through a browser.

Future Improvements

For the next version, I plan to develop a companion mobile app and optimize the internal wire management by design-specific routing channels into the 3D print. I also want to upgrade to a more powerful pump and add a nutrient tracking system. Finally, I’ll be adjusting my 3D printing settings to ensure a completely leak-proof reservoir design without needing secondary glue sealants.
